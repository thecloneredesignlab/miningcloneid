## Introduction

Whole-genome doubling (WGD) and aneuploidy are common features of cancer evolution, yet their prevalence varies substantially across biological contexts \cite{bielski_genome_2018,davoli_tumor_2017,salehi_clonal_2021. A central unresolved question is why high-ploidy states are tolerated in some environments but selected against in others \cite{nowicka_interactions_2025,beck_energetics_2025. One clue comes from the difference between cultured cancer cell lines and primary tumors: standard culture can relax nutrient, oxygen, stromal, and spatial constraints, whereas tumors experience heterogeneous and often limiting resource states. This suggests that the fitness effects of chromosome content are context-dependent rather than intrinsic constants.
A plausible basis for this context dependence is the energetic and homeostatic cost of increased chromosome content \cite{donovan_hyperdiploid_2014,nowicka_interactions_2025,beck_energetics_2025. WGD increases biosynthetic demand associated with DNA replication, RNA production, and protein synthesis. Aneuploidy further perturbs gene dosage and can generate proteotoxic, osmotic, oxidative, replication, and endoplasmic-reticulum stress \cite{santaguida_chromosome_2017,sheltzer_single-chromosome_2017,kuznetsova_chromosomal_2015. These stresses require compensatory responses, including protein quality control, degradation of excess proteins, DNA repair, and metabolic adaptation \cite{santaguida_chromosome_2017,sheltzer_single-chromosome_2017,kuznetsova_chromosomal_2015,beck_energetics_2025. High-ploidy cells are therefore expected to carry a resource-dependent fitness cost, especially when oxygen, glucose, amino acids, or other metabolic substrates become limiting.
Resource scarcity can also increase genomic instability \cite{nowicka_interactions_2025,beck_energetics_2025. Environmental stresses, including serum starvation, hypoxia, folate deficiency, and energy restriction, have been linked to karyotypic instability, chromosome missegregation, polyploidization, and increased cellular heterogeneity. The consequences of these events depend on ploidy: chromosome missegregation can trigger p53-mediated arrest, senescence, or death near diploidy, whereas WGD and high-ploidy states can buffer the relative dosage effect of individual chromosome gains or losses \cite{vitale_intratumor_2013,bakhoum_chromosomal_2012,thompson_proliferation_2010,sheltzer_single-chromosome_2017,silk_chromosome_2013,lopez_interplay_2020,elizalde_markov_2018,santaguida_chromosome_2017. Consistent with this view, ALFA-K inferred stronger fitness costs of missegregation near diploidy than near tetraploidy \cite{beck_alfa-k_2025. Thus, ploidy can determine whether CIN produces nonviable progeny or heritable karyotype variation.
Oxygen provides a tractable first-order axis for this resource landscape \cite{nowicka_interactions_2025,beck_energetics_2025,donovan_hyperdiploid_2014. However, oxygen is an imperfect surrogate for total resource availability. Tissue resource states also depend on glucose and amino-acid availability, perfusion, local consumption, waste accumulation, cell density, and tissue architecture. Therefore, the model developed here treats oxygen as an effective resource-stress variable: in vitro it is experimentally assigned, whereas in vivo it is a latent proxy inferred through the coupled tumor-growth and chromosome-number model.
Together, these observations identify a central tension in cancer ploidy evolution. Resource scarcity should select against WGD and high-ploidy states because additional chromosome content increases biosynthetic, proteostatic, and homeostatic demands. Yet adverse environments can also promote CIN, missegregation, and polyploidization, generating the variation on which selection acts. Whether this variation is eliminated or retained depends in part on ploidy-dependent daughter survival. We therefore developed a quantitative model that jointly represents resource-dependent proliferation, stress-associated death, stress-linked chromosome missegregation, WGD generation, and ploidy-dependent survival of altered progeny. We applied this model to matched near-diploid and near-tetraploid SUM159 lineages measured in vitro and in vivo to ask which ploidy-selective pressures are shared across environments and which are rewired by the tumor context.

## Methods


**Study design and data streams.**

We analyzed matched near-diploid and near-tetraploid SUM159 lineages across two experimental contexts. In vivo, orthotopic tumors initiated from near-diploid and near-tetraploid lineages were followed with longitudinal tumor-burden measurements and harvested for terminal single-cell chromosome-number profiling; where matched histology was available, necrosis was recorded. The in vivo calibration used the untreated subset of eight tumors, four initiated from each lineage. In vitro, the same lineage pair was analyzed in passage-structured culture experiments with measured passage growth, chromosome-number observations, and G0/G1 flow-density profiles under controlled oxygen conditions. This design varied both starting ploidy and environment, allowing the model to ask whether high-ploidy states are favored or disfavored depending on context.

**Model state and oxygen context.**

The model represents viable cells as a distribution over total chromosome-number states $N \in \{22,\ldots,154\$. It also tracks non-viable biomass in separate dead compartments, so simulated bulk tumor burden includes live cells and uncleared dead material, whereas terminal single-cell chromosome-number profiles sample only the viable population. In vivo, oxygen is modeled as an effective mean-field level that declines with chromosome-weighted tumor demand and relaxes toward a supply-demand target. In vitro, each passage segment is simulated at its assigned fixed oxygen level.

**Observation models.**

Longitudinal tumor burden constrains net growth, death, clearance, and resource-dependent proliferation. Terminal single-cell chromosome-number distributions constrain karyotype evolution, WGD, missegregation, and survival of altered progeny. Terminal necrosis was specified as an observation-model comparison to accumulated dead biomass. In vitro passage growth, chromosome-number observations, and flow-density profiles constrain proliferation and chromosome-number evolution under controlled oxygen histories.

**Separate and joint fitting.**

Separate in vivo and in vitro fits were optimized with independent random seeds. The joint fit used the same mechanistic model while evaluating separate transformed parameter vectors for the in vivo and in vitro likelihood components. Parameters representing the same biological mechanism were either hard-shared or soft-coupled across contexts. Soft-coupled parameters used a center--delta parameterization on the transformed scale,
z_Q^{\mathrm{vivo=c_Q+\frac{\delta_Q{2, \qquad z_Q^{\mathrm{vitro=c_Q-\frac{\delta_Q{2,
and a Welsch soft-coupling penalty discouraged unnecessary context separation. This construction allowed the fit to distinguish mechanisms that could remain shared across environments from mechanisms that required in vivo/in vitro divergence.

**Landscape preprocessing and joint warm starts.**

The joint-fitting preprocessing archive contained 500 separate in vivo best-fit parameter sets and 500 separate in vitro best-fit parameter sets. Prior-scaled parameter features were embedded by t-SNE and clustered. The in vivo landscape separated into three primary clusters ($k=3$, average silhouette 0.816), and the in vitro landscape separated into two primary clusters ($k=2$, average silhouette 0.774). Six joint warm starts were selected from in vivo cluster/subcluster representatives (`vi_C01_Sc01`, `vi_C01_Sc02`, `vi_C02_Sc01`, `vi_C02_Sc02`, `vi_C03_Sc01`, and `vi_C03_Sc02`) and paired with the same best separate in vitro fit (`seed10`). Each paired joint run was then optimized from 500 seeds; the delivered top-result archive retained the top 10 joint optimizer seeds per warm-start pair.

**Fixed-O2 attractor classification.**

For each of the 500 separate in vivo fits, the fixed-O2 generator was evaluated analytically across a dense oxygen grid from 0 to 5\% O2. The dominant eigenvector was used to compute long-run dominant mean ploidy at each oxygen value. Curves were classified by shape as complex nonmonotone, inverted-U, monotone-increasing, u-shaped, plateau-type, or monotone-decreasing, and spectral-gap diagnostics were used to flag unreliable or cautionary regions. This analysis was used to characterize plausible O2--CIN--ploidy response regimes rather than to impose a single response shape.

## Results


### Matched culture and tumor trajectories motivate a resource-stress model of ploidy evolution

The matched SUM159 design placed near-diploid and near-tetraploid lineages on a common interpretive axis across culture and tumor settings (Figure \ref{fig:matched-data). The key biological motivation was that chromosome-number trajectories changed in both environments, but the two settings differed in time scale, oxygen control, and measurement structure. The model therefore represented resource stress as a latent or assigned axis that can simultaneously suppress proliferation, increase death, increase chromosome missegregation, generate WGD-like states, and filter the survival of altered daughters.

### Separate in vitro fits support oxygen-dependent chromosome-number remodeling rather than simple high-ploidy killing

The best separate in vitro fit was `seed10`, with objective 3.8525; the top 10 in vitro fits were numerically tight, spanning only 0.0098 objective units (0.254\% relative spread). All top 10 in vitro seeds mapped to the `vt_C02_Sc02` subcluster from the preprocessing landscape, indicating that the retained low-objective in vitro solutions were concentrated in one region of the 500-seed landscape.
In the best in vitro seed, oxygen deprivation suppressed predicted passage growth and broadened chromosome-number distributions. In the 2N deprived branch, the predicted mean chromosome number increased from 46.7 at the early matched point to 66.7 at the final retained deprived snapshot, while the distribution became broad and high-ploidy enriched: by passage 38, q25 remained 44, q75 reached 85, and q99 reached 107. Thus, the fitted trajectory is not simply direct elimination of high-ploidy cells. Instead, the model predicts coexistence of near-diploid and high-ploidy fractions under prolonged deprivation.
The 4N deprived branch showed a different pattern. Its predicted mean declined from 84.7 at the early matched point to 80.8 at the final deprived snapshot, with the distribution still centered broadly in high-ploidy states. This supports a modest oxygen-associated downward shift from the 4N state without requiring wholesale high-ploidy removal. A limitation is that one late 2N deprived observed replicate had a mean chromosome number of 88.1, whereas the shared model segment predicted 64.3. We therefore interpret the in vitro fit as supporting oxygen-dependent remodeling and broadening, not exact replicate-level reconstruction of every high-ploidy observation.

### Separate in vivo fits support terminal convergence toward lower chromosome-number states but not one unique oxygen-response law

The best separate in vivo fit was `seed25`, with objective 14.1193; the retained top 10 separate in vivo fits were also tight, spanning 0.0555 objective units (0.393\% relative spread). The best seed predicted terminal viable chromosome-number distributions near the diploid range. Across the four 2N-initiated tumors, the mean observed terminal chromosome number was 44.3 and the predicted mean was 46.1. Across the four 4N-initiated tumors, the mean observed terminal chromosome number was 50.0 and the predicted means were 48.9--50.8 depending on harvest group. Predicted high-ploidy terminal mass ($N\ge 66$) was small, averaging 0.6\% for 2N-initiated tumors and 2.2\% for 4N-initiated tumors. These fits therefore support the conclusion that the in vivo context favors lower chromosome-number states relative to the starting 4N lineage.
However, the in vivo landscape did not resolve a single fixed-O2 response shape. Across all 500 separate in vivo seeds, 261 seeds (52.2\%) were classified as complex nonmonotone, 149 (29.8\%) as inverted-U shaped, 57 (11.4\%) as monotone-increasing, 25 (5.0\%) as u-shaped, five (1.0\%) as single-transition increase-then-plateau, and three (0.6\%) as monotone-decreasing. Among the top 25 objective-ranked in vivo seeds, response classes remained mixed: 40\% inverted-U, 36\% complex nonmonotone, 16\% u-shaped, and 8\% monotone-increasing. Thus, oxygen alone should not be interpreted as selecting a unique monotonic ploidy response in the current model. Instead, the fixed-O2 analysis identifies several plausible O2--CIN--ploidy regimes that remain compatible with low objective values.

### Joint soft-coupled fits identify context-specific proliferation, CIN, and survival-filter axes

The joint analysis paired six in vivo landscape-informed warm starts with the same best in vitro seed. The best joint objective came from `fit_joint_tsne_vi_seed366_C01Sc01_vt_seed10/seed472`, with objective 18.8523. The next best warm-start pairs were `vi_seed322_C02Sc02` (18.8901) and `vi_seed25_C02Sc01` (18.9705), whereas `vi_seed311_C03Sc02`, `vi_seed290_C01Sc02`, and `vi_seed138_C03Sc01` gave higher best objectives. This ordering indicates that joint likelihood favors a subset of the in vivo parameter landscape but does not eliminate all landscape uncertainty.
The best joint seed resolved strong context separation in several soft-coupled parameters. The effective proliferative ceiling was much lower in vivo than in vitro: $\lambda_{\max^{\mathrm{vivo=0.239$ versus $\lambda_{\max^{\mathrm{vitro=1.349$ (ratio 0.177). Stress-linked missegregation was higher in vivo: $p_{\mathrm{misseg^{\mathrm{vivo=0.117$ versus 0.0106 in vitro (ratio 11.1). The stress-associated death scale was also higher in vivo: $\mu_{\mathrm{hp^{\mathrm{vivo=0.0513$ versus 0.0050 in vitro (ratio 10.3). Across the best seed from each of the six warm-start pairs, the median context ratios remained directionally consistent: median in vivo/in vitro ratios were 0.177 for $\lambda_{\max$, 16.8 for $p_{\mathrm{misseg$, 16.6 for $\mu_{\mathrm{hp$, 4.43 for $\alpha_{O_2$, and 1.70 for $\gamma_{\mathrm{growth$.
The post-missegregation survival filter also differed across contexts. In the best joint seed, in vivo $s_{\max$ was lower than in vitro (0.909 versus 1.000), $\beta_{\mathrm{buf$ was lower in vivo (0.165 versus 1.591), and $n_{\mathrm{exp$ was higher in vivo (10.0 versus 3.16). These parameters shape how daughter survival changes with chromosome-number state. Because several estimates lie at or near bounds, the appropriate interpretation is not that individual fitted values are direct biological measurements; rather, the joint fit identifies proliferation, stress-linked CIN, and daughter-survival filtering as the main context-sensitive axes required to reconcile matched culture and tumor data.

### Necrosis outputs require repair before serving as independent evidence

Although the observation model included terminal necrosis, the delivered top-result files do not contain finite predicted necrosis fractions. Across the 10 retained separate in vivo seeds, all 60 necrosis rows contained observed necrosis fractions but zero finite predicted fractions. Across the 60 retained joint top-result seeds, all 360 necrosis rows likewise had zero finite predicted necrosis fractions. The objective summaries still report a necrosis component, so this appears to be an implementation or export inconsistency rather than absence of observed necrosis data. We therefore do not use necrosis predictions as independent support in the present manuscript text. Before submission, finite predicted necrosis values should be exported and reconciled with the likelihood contribution, or necrosis should be described as an intended but unaudited component of the current output package.

## Discussion

This study integrates matched in vitro and in vivo ploidy-evolution datasets into a single mechanistic framework. The central result is that culture and tumor settings can be described by the same model structure but require different context-specific parameter values. In the current fits, the in vivo environment is represented by a lower effective proliferative ceiling, stronger stress-linked chromosome missegregation, and a different post-missegregation survival filter. These are model-resolved effects of the tumor context, not direct measurements of individual environmental variables.
The separate in vitro results support oxygen-dependent chromosome-number remodeling without a simple high-ploidy killing mechanism. The 2N deprived branch broadens substantially and becomes high-ploidy enriched, whereas the 4N deprived branch shifts downward modestly while remaining broadly high-ploidy. The separate in vivo results support terminal convergence toward lower chromosome-number states, especially for tumors initiated from the 4N lineage. Together, these findings are consistent with the hypothesis that resource-stress environments can both generate chromosome-number variation and filter which altered progeny survive.
The fixed-O2 landscape analysis is equally important because it constrains the strength of the biological conclusion. Low-objective in vivo fits do not select one unique monotone relationship between oxygen and long-run ploidy. Instead, the 500-seed analysis supports multiple response classes, many with small spectral-gap cautions. Thus, the current model should be presented as defining a family of plausible oxygen-CIN-ploidy regimes. Further discrimination among these regimes will require additional data that directly constrain oxygen, stress timing, CIN rates, or longitudinal chromosome-number distributions.
Several limitations should be addressed before submission. First, necrosis predictions are not finite in the delivered top-result tables, even though necrosis is included in the stated observation model and objective summaries. This must be repaired before necrosis can be used as an independent validation stream. Second, the 2N deprived in vitro fit captures broad distributional remodeling but does not fully match one late high-ploidy replicate. Third, many joint soft-coupled parameters approach bounds or show saturated penalty behavior, so the manuscript should emphasize qualitative axes and robustness across warm starts rather than overinterpreting exact natural-scale parameter values. Fourth, effective oxygen in vivo remains a latent proxy for broader resource stress; it should not be interpreted as direct measured tissue oxygen.
Despite these limitations, the current results support a coherent hypothesis: resource context does not simply favor or eliminate WGD. Instead, it rewires the balance between proliferation, chromosome missegregation, WGD generation, and ploidy-dependent survival of altered daughters. This framework explains why the same lineage pair can show different chromosome-number dynamics in culture and tumors, and it provides a testable set of hypotheses for future experiments that measure oxygen/resource exposure, CIN, and ploidy-resolved viability more directly.
