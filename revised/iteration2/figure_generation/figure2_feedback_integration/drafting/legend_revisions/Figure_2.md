## Figure 2. Model architecture: death-hazard-linked chromosome instability couples resource limitation to ploidy adaptation

**(A)** Resource limitation, modeled through O2, lowers chromosome-state-specific proliferation and raises the chromosome-state-specific death hazard. The death hazard serves as the model's latent measure of experienced cellular stress; the stress-associated increment in missegregation is linked to this hazard rather than directly to O2, while baseline missegregation and whole-genome doubling (WGD) remain separate model components. Post-missegregation (post-MS) survival and state-dependent fitness filter altered daughters; movement toward better-adapted chromosome states can lower population-average death and chromosome instability (CIN) even at fixed environmental O2. O2 is assigned by passage in vitro and is latent and demand-dependent in vivo. **(B)** Schematics show the encoded O2 dependence of proliferation and death for lower- and higher-chromosome-number states; the direct fitness cost is stronger at higher N. These are model relationships, not fitted trajectories. **(C)** Missegregation produces correlated complementary daughter shifts, N-m and N+m. For the same |m|, daughters from a higher-N mother have greater modeled survival, illustrating ploidy-dependent buffering. All panels describe model architecture rather than measurements or fitted results.
