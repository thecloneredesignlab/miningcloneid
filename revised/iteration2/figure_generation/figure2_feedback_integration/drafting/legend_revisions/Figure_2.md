## Figure 2. Model architecture: death-linked chromosome instability couples resource limitation to ploidy adaptation

**(A)** Resource limitation, represented through O2, lowers chromosome-state-specific proliferation and raises the chromosome-state-specific death hazard used as the model's measure of experienced cellular stress. The effective per-chromosome missegregation probability increases with this death hazard, whereas whole-genome doubling (WGD) remains a separate constant-probability branch per division. Missegregated daughters are filtered by post-missegregation (post-MS) survival, and all viable chromosome states are further filtered by state-dependent proliferation and death. A shift toward better-adapted chromosome states can then lower population-average death and, consequently, population-average chromosome instability (CIN), even if environmental O2 remains fixed. O2 is prescribed by passage in vitro and is a latent, demand-dependent model variable in vivo. **(B)** Schematic encoded dependencies of effective proliferation and stress-associated death on O2 for lower- and higher-chromosome-number states. Under resource limitation, the direct fitness cost is stronger at higher N. The curves illustrate model structure and are not fitted trajectories. **(C)** A chromosome-missegregation event produces correlated complementary daughter shifts, N-m and N+m. For the same |m|, altered daughters from a higher-N mother have greater modeled survival than daughters from a lower-N mother, illustrating ploidy-dependent buffering. All panels describe the model architecture rather than experimental measurements or fitted results.
