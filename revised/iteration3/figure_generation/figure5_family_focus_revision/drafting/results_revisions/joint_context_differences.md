## Results Text

To determine which culture-tumor differences persisted across fitted solution regions, we jointly estimated 14 matched parameter pairs under soft coupling. Each of the three in-vivo landscape families contributed two candidate warm-start pairs with the common seed-10 in-vitro anchor, giving six pairs and 3,000 searches; Figure 5 shows one prespecified primary pair per family. All three families were retained because the fitted landscape did not identify a unique biological solution. C01 yielded the lowest individual full objective (18.852), C01 and C02 formed the leading fit tier, and C02 was more robust to representative choice; C03 fit less well overall but remained a biologically distinct alternative. We therefore use C01 as the biological worked example because it provided the best individual fit, while using C02 and C03 to identify conclusions that did not depend on that choice. This operational focus does not identify C01 as the uniquely correct family.

The three primary winners reproduced the principal in-vitro growth and chromosome-number observables and the in-vivo burden and terminal chromosome-number scales, with residual deviations for individual culture chromosome counts. Across the 500-endpoint primary-pair ensembles, the most defensible joint interpretation had three parts. First, culture supported a higher proliferative ceiling. Second, tumors translated experienced resource stress more strongly into ploidy-dependent growth suppression, death, and inducible chromosome missegregation. Third, the maximum post-missegregation survival, or buffering, ceiling was approximately shared, but the shape of the survival function differed between contexts. By contrast, the direction of fitted WGD probability varied among families, so the joint fits did not support a context-wide directional conclusion about WGD generation.

An important distinction between contexts is that O2 was experimentally imposed in vitro while other medium resources were not modeled as limiting, whereas fitted low O2 in tumors is a latent coordinate that may mark a broader perfusion- and demand-linked resource deficit. Stronger in-vivo growth, death, and inducible-missegregation couplings should therefore be interpreted as effective responses to composite resource stress rather than necessarily as intrinsically stronger oxygen-specific pathways. The shared model coordinate permits standardized function comparisons, but an identical numerical O2 value does not imply a biologically equivalent resource state in culture and tumors.

Under this interpretation, C01 provides a particularly informative example of shared cellular machinery operating in different resource ecologies. Baseline per-chromosome missegregation and fitted response steepness were approximately equal between contexts. The C01 contrast therefore did not require higher baseline per-chromosome missegregation or a sharper response in tumors. Instead, it placed the principal difference downstream, in the magnitude and consequences of experienced resource stress; the fitted effective O2 threshold nevertheless remained context-specific. Consistent with this interpretation, C01 had a maximal proliferation rate of 0.239 day^-1 in vivo versus 1.35 day^-1 in vitro, and its complete in-vivo proliferation curve remained below the culture curve at both 2N and 4N.

Complete effective per-chromosome missegregation combines baseline per-chromosome missegregation with a death-hazard-linked inducible increment. Despite C01's approximately shared baseline probability, complete effective missegregation was 11.1-fold higher in vivo at both 44 and 88 chromosomes at a standardized model input of 0% O2, and 7.42- and 10.4-fold higher, respectively, at 1% O2. C02 and C03 preserved the same low-O2 direction: across all three families, the in-vivo-to-in-vitro ratio at standardized 0% O2 ranged from 11.1 to 20.7 at 44 chromosomes and from 11.2 to 20.6 at 88 chromosomes. These comparisons evaluate both fitted functions at common values of the model's O2 coordinate; they do not imply biologically equivalent 0% or 1% oxygen environments. Within that standardized comparison, the higher complete effective per-chromosome missegregation in vivo arose through amplification of the death-hazard-linked inducible branch rather than requiring higher baseline per-chromosome missegregation in C01.

The approximately shared maximum buffering ceiling likewise did not imply a shared survival function. In C01, per-copy post-missegregation survival was 0.770 in vivo versus 0.204 in vitro at 44 chromosomes and 0.908 versus 0.837 at 88 chromosomes. C02 and C03 preserved both directions: across the three families, in-vivo survival ranged from 0.728 to 0.819 at 44 chromosomes and from 0.904 to 0.914 at 88 chromosomes. Tumors therefore had a higher survival floor. Conversely, survival increased by only 0.138 between 44 and 88 chromosomes in C01 in vivo, compared with 0.633 in vitro, and the in-vivo increase remained smaller in C02 and C03. Culture therefore had the steeper ploidy-dependent survival slope. Within the modeled architecture, the elevated in-vivo survival near diploidy can retain altered daughters, including chromosome-loss descendants, while stronger growth and death penalties act against chromosome-rich states. These cooperating features provide a model-consistent route by which missegregation can supply viable lower-chromosome descendants that are then favored under broader resource limitation; the fitted functions do not by themselves establish that this route caused the faster and deeper reduction of 4N-derived tumors.

All 3,000 searches completed, and all six warm-start pairs were retained for the subsequent fixed-input response analysis. The inferred contrasts remain conditional on the common in-vitro anchor, parameter bounds, and bounded soft-coupling penalty. Within that scope, however, all three families supported a higher culture proliferative ceiling, stronger tumor conversion of experienced resource stress into growth suppression, death, and inducible missegregation, and a shared buffering ceiling with context-dependent survival shape. C01 further showed that these differences can arise while baseline per-chromosome missegregation and response steepness remain approximately shared. Higher baseline missegregation, altered response steepness, and context-specific WGD probability are therefore family-specific refinements rather than requirements of the common joint-fit interpretation.

## Revision Notes

1. C01 is used as the worked example because it has the lowest individual full objective, not because the analysis identifies it as the uniquely correct biological family.
2. C01 and C02 remain the leading objective tier; C02 is more robust to representative choice, and C03 remains a lower-fitting but biologically distinct alternative.
3. Approximate equality of baseline per-chromosome missegregation and response steepness applies to C01, not to all three families.
4. The fitted WGD-probability direction varies among families and is not assigned a context-wide interpretation.
5. Identical numerical O2 values are standardized model-coordinate comparisons, not biologically equivalent culture and tumor resource environments.
6. The chromosome-loss route is an interpretation of the fitted functions and encoded transition structure, not a measured directional flux.
